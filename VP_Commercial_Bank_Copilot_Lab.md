# Immersion Experience - Executive Banking Workflow

Use Microsoft 365 Copilot to prepare for an executive review, analyze a forecast workbook, and draft a concise leadership update.

You will complete three tasks:

- Prepare for an executive review using **Copilot Chat**
- Analyze a forecast workbook using **Copilot in Excel**
- Draft a leadership update using **Copilot in Word** or **Copilot Chat**

> **NOTE:** The prompts are starting points. Replace bracketed text with your own topic, file, meeting, or audience. Keep prompts simple, review Copilot's sources, and validate important facts before using the output.

## Scenario

You are a VP in a commercial bank preparing for a leadership review. You need to understand the business context, identify risks, review forecast trends, and create a short update for senior leaders.

## Task 1: Prepare for an executive review

Use **Copilot Chat** to create a short briefing for a leadership meeting.

### Steps

1. Open a browser and go to [Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat](https://m365copilot.com/).
2. Use work mode if available.
3. Reference a relevant meeting, document, email thread, or topic.
4. Ask Copilot for a concise executive briefing.
5. Review the response and check any cited sources.

### Sample prompt

```text
Prepare me for my next leadership review about [topic]. Summarize the key context, decisions needed, risks, and questions I should ask.
```

### Follow-up prompt

```text
Make this briefing shorter and organize it into: Key Takeaway, Risks, Decisions Needed, and Next Steps.
```

## Task 2: Analyze a forecast workbook

Use **Copilot in Excel** to identify business trends and risks in a sample forecast workbook.

### Steps

1. Open the sample workbook: [aka.ms/sampleExcel2](https://aka.ms/sampleExcel2).
2. Open the workbook in Excel for the web or desktop Excel.
3. Confirm the data is formatted as a table.
4. Open Copilot in Excel.
5. Ask Copilot to identify trends, risks, and recommended actions.
6. Validate the numbers and assumptions before using the output.

### Sample prompt

```text
Analyze this workbook for an executive. Identify the biggest revenue, expense, and margin trends, and show the evidence for each.
```

### Follow-up prompt

```text
Find the areas with the highest margin pressure. Summarize what is driving the issue and suggest three leadership actions.
```

### Optional prompt

```text
Create an executive summary of this forecast with the top three insights, risks to watch, and questions for the finance team.
```

## Task 3: Draft a leadership update

Use **Copilot in Word** or **Copilot Chat** to turn your findings into a concise leadership update.

### Steps

1. Open [word.new](https://word.new) or stay in Copilot Chat.
2. Use the meeting briefing and forecast insights from Tasks 1 and 2.
3. Draft a short update for a senior leadership audience.
4. Refine the tone and shorten the message.
5. Confirm that the final version is accurate and appropriate to share.

### Sample prompt

```text
Draft a concise leadership update based on these findings. Use four sections: Progress, Risks, Decisions Needed, and Next Steps.
```

### Follow-up prompt

```text
Rewrite this for a senior executive audience. Make it shorter, clearer, and focused on decisions and next steps.
```

## Optional Task 4: Use a Chief of Staff agent

If a **Chief of Staff** agent is available, use it to create an executive-ready summary.

### Sample prompt

```text
Prepare me for an executive review. Summarize the top issues, risks, decisions needed, owners, and next steps from the available context.
```

### Follow-up prompt

```text
Turn this into a concise executive briefing with decisions needed, owners, and follow-up actions.
```

## Wrap-up

Before using the final output, check:

- Are the facts accurate?
- Are the sources appropriate?
- Are risks and assumptions clearly labeled?
- Are decisions and next steps explicit?
- Is the message short enough for a senior audience?

## Prompt pattern

Use this structure when creating your own prompts:

```text
Goal: [what you want]
Context: [why it matters and who the audience is]
Sources: [specific file, email, meeting, or workbook]
Expectations: [format, length, tone, and sections]
```
